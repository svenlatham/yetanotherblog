Last Friday I spent the day in [Old Broadcasting House](http://www.oldbroadcastinghouse.com/), Leeds with a group of like-minded people interested in starting, or already on their freelance journey.  
The event, organised by [Stephanie Booth](http://climbtothestars.org/) was originally intended to be a larger conference but due to disappointing numbers it was cancelled. The attendees were instead invited to a smaller, less formal gathering called [SoloCamp](http://going-solo.net/2008/09/13/happy-soloists-at-solocamp-leeds/).  
The twenty-or-so of us who attended spent the day talking about a variety of issues: [Finance](http://going-solo.pbwiki.com/SoloCamp%20Leeds:%20Finance), [Confidence](http://going-solo.pbwiki.com/SoloCamp%20Leeds:%20Confidence), [Growth](http://going-solo.pbwiki.com/SoloCamp%20Leeds:%20Growth) and [Clients](http://going-solo.pbwiki.com/SoloCamp%20Leeds:%20Clients). The full overview of the day can be found on the [SoloCamp wiki](http://going-solo.pbwiki.com/SoloCamp%20Leeds).  
This was my first "[BarCamp](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcamp)" style event, and I took the opportunity to get some presentational confidence by moderating the Growth session. I had a bit of a stumble with the session as my ad-hoc material quickly ran out, but thankfully the session turned into a rather interesting discussion about [Hype Cycles](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hype_cycle) towards the end (thanks to [Dennis](http://www.accmanpro.com/)!).  
All in all, a very interesting and educational experience. It gave me renewed enthusiasm about getting [my company](http://www.latham-it.co.uk/) up and properly running. It also had me thinking about charging policies, marketing and finance, all of which are very important to a vulnerable technology start-up. More generally, it has been a great pleasure meeting a variety of individuals, from various backgrounds who I hope to keep in touch with.
