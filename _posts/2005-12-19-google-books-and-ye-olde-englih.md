Poking around [Google Books](http://books.google.com/) (formerly Google Print) this morning and discovered an interesting "oddity".  
  
In old English texts, the lower-case letter 's' when appearing at the start or within a word was written as a sort-of 'f' character - more specifically, [ſ](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_s)  
  
Turns out that Google Books can't cope with this - it reads these as the letter 'f', so when searching old texts be sure to accommodate this - for example, searching for [impoffible](http://books.google.com/books?ie=UTF-8&hl=en&id=3qPmwC4gXrYC&q=impoffible+date:1700-1800&pg=PA134&img=1&zoom=3&sig=pefaQGRzjm9ACDg_k1XxppdCkLA) will work and highlight the correct words, but [impossible](http://books.google.com/books?ie=UTF-8&hl=en&id=3qPmwC4gXrYC&q=impossible+date:1700-1800&pg=PA134&img=1&zoom=3&sig=pefaQGRzjm9ACDg_k1XxppdCkLA) won't.  
  
It's hardly an earth-shattering bug, but it's an interesting note to Google and other would-be book search services to check their OCR software is compatible with 18th century texts!
